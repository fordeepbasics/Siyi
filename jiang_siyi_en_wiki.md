'''Jiang Siyi''' ({{zh|c=姜思毅|p=Jiāng Sīyì}}; 1920 – 1 January 2009), born '''Jiang Yaozeng''' ({{zh|c=姜耀曾}}), was a Chinese military officer, theorist, and historian of the [[People's Liberation Army]] (PLA) who held the rank of [[Lieutenant general (China)|lieutenant general]]. He served as Deputy Commandant of the [[PLA Academy of Military Science]] (AMS), Vice President of the [[PLA Political Academy]], and Director of the Propaganda Department of the [[General Political Department of the People's Liberation Army|General Political Department]] (GPD).

During the 1980s and 1990s, when the PLA shifted its core doctrine from preparing for imminent general war to peacetime modernization and regional contingencies, Jiang played a prominent role in guiding Chinese military historiography and theory. He was the chief editor of seminal official military publications, including the six-volume ''Seventy-Year History of the PLA Political Work'' (1991), the authoritative official battle history ''History of the Self-Defensive Counterattack Operations on the Sino-Indian Border'' (1994), and the ''Dictionary of Major Events of the Chinese People's Liberation Army'' (1992).<ref name="Shambaugh">{{cite book |last=Shambaugh |first=David |title=Modernizing China's Military: Progress, Problems, and Prospects |publisher=[[University of California Press]] |year=2004 |pages=112–115 |isbn=978-0-520-24238-8}}</ref><ref name="USACGSC">{{cite book |last=Garver |first=John W. |title=Protracted Contest: Sino-Indian Rivalry in the Twentieth Century |publisher=[[University of Washington Press]] |year=2001 |pages=58–62 |isbn=978-0-295-98073-7}}</ref>

== Early life and revolutionary activities ==
Jiang Siyi was born in [[Tianjin]] in 1920. During his student years, he became involved in patriotic anti-imperialist student activism following the Japanese encroachments in North China. In December 1935, while studying at Tianjin Fulun Middle School, he participated in the [[December 9th Movement]], organizing student protests and anti-Japanese boycotts.<ref name="Xinhua">{{cite news |title=姜思毅同志逝世 (Comrade Jiang Siyi passed away) |publisher=[[Xinhua News Agency]] |date=20 January 2009 |url=http://news.sina.com.cn/c/2009-01-20/225017084931.shtml |access-date=10 September 2026 |language=zh}}</ref>

In 1936, Jiang was introduced into the [[Chinese Communist Party]] (CCP) by [[Yao Yilin]] (who later became Vice Premier of the State Council). Jiang subsequently served as the secretary of the underground CCP branch at Fulun Middle School, leading youth mobilization in Tianjin.<ref name="Xinhua" />

== Military career ==

=== Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945) ===
Following the outbreak of the full-scale [[Second Sino-Japanese War]] in July 1937, Jiang joined the 1st Detachment of the East Hebei Anti-Japanese Allied Army in October 1937. He subsequently transitioned into the [[Eighth Route Army]], being assigned to the 115th Division as head of detachment propaganda.<ref name="Xinhua" />

In 1938, Jiang was appointed head of the propaganda and education section of the Eighth Route Army's Eastward Advance Anti-Japanese Column led by [[Xiao Hua (general)|Xiao Hua]], conducting guerrilla political mobilization across the Hebei-Shandong border region. In 1939, he was transferred to serve as deputy director of propaganda for the Luxi Military District and the 343rd Brigade of the 115th Division. Later in the war, he served as director of propaganda for the Hebei-Shandong-Henan (Jiluyu) Military District, developing protocols for wartime ideological mobilization, morale sustenance under counter-guerrilla blockades, and psychological warfare targeting Japanese and collaborationist forces.<ref name="BaikeBio">{{cite book |editor=Military History Research Department of the Academy of Military Science |title=中国人民解放军大事典 (Dictionary of Major Events of the Chinese People's Liberation Army) |publisher=Tianjin People's Publishing House |year=1992 |pages=1230 |isbn=978-7-201-01046-5 |language=zh}}</ref>

=== Chinese Civil War (1945–1949) ===
During the [[Chinese Civil War]], Jiang served in political leadership roles within the Shanxi-Hebei-Shandong-Henan Field Army and later the [[Second Field Army]]:
* In November 1945, he was appointed director of the political department of the 21st Brigade, 7th Column. During the Battle of Zhangfengji (章缝集) in the 1946 Juye Campaign against the Nationalist 11th Reorganized Division, he organized and participated in forward frontline assault operations.<ref>{{cite book |last=Jiang |first=Siyi |title=生命线的探索 (Exploration of the Lifeline) |publisher=People's Publishing House |year=1993 |language=zh}}</ref>
* In 1947, he was named director of the political department of the 19th Brigade and later the 1st Brigade of the 1st Column. Under the command of [[Liu Bocheng]] and [[Deng Xiaoping]], he participated in the [[Dabie Mountains Campaign|advance into the Dabie Mountains]], maintaining field political discipline without rear supply bases.
* Between 1948 and 1949, Jiang participated in the [[Huaihai Campaign]]—including the encirclement of General [[Huang Wei]]'s 12th Army Group at Shuangduiji—and the subsequent [[Yangtze River Crossing Campaign]].
* In early 1949, he was appointed deputy political commissar of the 46th Division, and subsequently political commissar of the 48th Division of the 16th Army (5th Corps, Second Field Army). He led division forces in the [[Southwest Campaign (1949–1950)|Southwest Campaign]], taking part in the takeover of southern [[Sichuan]] and concurrently serving as the first CCP committee secretary of [[Luzhou]] prefecture.<ref name="Xinhua" />

=== People's Republic of China ===
Following the establishment of the People's Republic of China, Jiang served as deputy political commissar of the 5th Branch of Southwest Military and Political University. He was subsequently reassigned to the central PLA leadership in Beijing, holding positions within the [[General Political Department of the People's Liberation Army|General Political Department]] (GPD), including section chief (1950), deputy secretary-general (1955), and director of the GPD Propaganda Department (1958). In September 1955, when the PLA adopted military ranks, he was awarded the rank of [[Senior Colonel]] and conferred the [[Order of Independence and Freedom]] (2nd Class) and [[Order of Liberation]] (2nd Class).<ref name="Xinhua" />

In late 1960, in the political aftermath of the 1959 Lushan Conference and the 1960 Enlarged Meeting of the Central Military Commission, Jiang was implicated in the alleged "Tan Zheng Anti-Party Clique" alongside GPD Director [[Tan Zheng]], resulting in his administrative demotion. During the early [[Cultural Revolution]], he was further targeted by radical factions around [[Jiang Qing]] and sent down to manual labor, later serving as deputy director of the political department of the Guangzhou Military Region Production and Construction Corps in [[Guangdong]].

Following his political rehabilitation in the late 1970s, Jiang was appointed deputy dean of studies and later Vice President of the [[PLA Political Academy]]. There, he led the reconstruction of political education curricula and directed the compilation of the first post-Mao standardized military political academy textbook, ''History of Political Work in the Chinese People's Liberation Army'' (1984).<ref name="Swaine">{{cite book |last=Swaine |first=Michael D. |title=The Military & Political Succession in China |publisher=[[RAND Corporation]] |year=1992 |pages=105–108 |isbn=978-0-8330-1296-8}}</ref>

=== Academy of Military Science and later life ===
In late 1985, Jiang was appointed Deputy Commandant of the [[PLA Academy of Military Science]] (AMS). Under the strategic reorientation initiated by Deng Xiaoping, which transitioned the military posture to peacetime professionalization and preparation for "local wars under modern conditions", Jiang oversaw extensive strategic research, military historiography projects, and the compilation of major war records. He also supervised the production of the documentary series ''The War to Resist U.S. Aggression and Aid Korea''.<ref name="Xinhua" />

In September 1988, when the PLA restored military rank designations, Jiang was awarded the rank of [[Lieutenant general (China)|Lieutenant General]] and received the [[Order of the Red Star (China)|Red Star Meritorious Honor Medal (2nd Class)]].<ref name="Xinhua" />

Jiang retired from active service in 1998. In retirement, he served as vice president of the Chinese Communist Party History Society, advisor to the Association of National History of the PRC, and executive vice president of the China Yan Huang Culture Research Association.

Jiang died in Beijing on 1 January 2009 at the age of 89.<ref name="Xinhua" />
